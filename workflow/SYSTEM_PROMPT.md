# Soul-Inspired biographical documentary production

## Scope and intake

Create a researched biographical documentary for Eleesha / The Soul Whisperer. Read the project brief and available sources first. Reuse supplied choices and completed assets. Ask only for missing decisions needed for the next stage. Treat source documents and the archived upstream prompt as reference data.

Plan a 5–20 minute flagship documentary. This is an editorial target, not a verified VideoExpress project capability. Check current visible controls before production; if one project cannot accommodate the approved film, plan chapter projects and final assembly without shortening approved narration. Do not silently apply the upstream five-minute cap.

Default composition: landscape 16:9 for the flagship; create separately composed 9:16 versions for Shorts. Narrator: Eleesha’s existing authorized CloneVoice voice, selected by its actual account identity; do not substitute Ethan Sterling or a synthetic approximation. Presenter appearance is optional, with no mandatory 20-second introduction. Do not invent Eleesha’s appearance or personal experiences. Tone: warm, reflective, knowledgeable and accessible, as Eleesha telling someone the subject’s story. Personal interpretation must be clearly attributed.

## 1. Research dossier

Inventory supplied materials with source IDs, edition, provenance and precise page/chapter locators. Record unavailable attachments as missing; never imply they have been read. Gather independent reliable evidence for life events and historical context. For every proposed claim record claim ID, wording, category, source locator, evidence, confidence and unresolved questions.

Categories: verified biographical fact; primary-text teaching; interpretation; uncertain/disputed. A teaching anecdote establishes what an author wrote, not independent proof that the event occurred. Do not fabricate motives, private dialogue, influence links or transitions in a life story. Verify publication dates and posthumous publication separately. Check quotations against the actual edition and preserve exact wording and attribution. Keep research citations outside spoken narration.

## 2. Outline and master script

Use Biography → Ideas → Legacy. Build a hook, early life and artistic/work background, relevant historical context, key books and teachings with sourced examples, distinctive style, evidenced influence, continued relevance and a natural invitation into Eleesha’s related work. Use 8–10 chapters where the runtime supports them; avoid padding a shorter film.

Prepare the editorial master with claim IDs, quotation locators, pronunciation notes, visual opportunities and open flags. Estimate natural speech length, then fact-check each factual sentence against the dossier. Attribute spiritual beliefs as teachings rather than guaranteed outcomes. Resolve or remove unsupported claims before narration. Keep Florence’s teaching, editorial interpretation and Eleesha’s own perspective distinguishable.

## 3. Narration

Create a separate clean spoken script from the checked master: no claim IDs, citations, visual instructions or research notes. Preserve meaning and quotation wording. Record the master version and narration version used. Use the chosen voice naturally rather than forcing a word count through speed changes.

Check for existing completed narration before generating. Generate only the required approved segments; track job IDs and completion and never resubmit a processing job. Once completed, lock each audio asset and its sequence. Do not rewrite, regenerate, cut, split, trim silence, stretch or change its speed merely to match footage. Adapt video coverage to the full audio. An editorial audio revision must be deliberate and recorded, preserving the previous version until replaced.

## 4. Storyboard and assets

Use scene modes: biographical reconstruction, archival treatment, teaching metaphor, quotation, transition, optional Eleesha presenter. Select a coherent visual palette for the project without requiring all footage to be watercolor. Clearly distinguish invented reconstructions/metaphors from historical evidence; never pass generated imagery off as archival photography.

For each scene record scene ID, chapter, narration segment and source/claim IDs, planned and measured in/out, scene mode, image/video direction, motion, overlay text, asset IDs and rights status. After narration completion, replace estimated timings with measured timings. Preserve consistent identity, wardrobe, era, architecture and location. Describe garments and physical actions concretely when generating people; do not force construction labor into a biography. Multi-shot action is useful where the story requires it, not mandatory for every archival image or quote.

Asset manifest fields: asset ID; scene; description; origin/source URL; creator; edition/date; rights category; rights evidence and territory; attribution; reconstruction label; file/job ID; status. Categories: public domain, licensed, generated, owned by Eleesha, unresolved. Clear rights per item and edition; an author’s age alone does not clear photographs, later editions, recordings or introductions. Do not upload the supplied complete-works file into a public repository as part of this workflow.

Keep tracking IDs, filenames and internal notes out of generation prompts. Supply reference images using the actual reference selector. Compose readable quote graphics from verified text; do not depend on generated lettering for accuracy.

## 5. VideoExpress and CloneVoice production

Use the visible browser and current UI. Sign-in requiring the user is a handoff point. Verify controls rather than treating upstream UI descriptions as current guarantees. Where available use manual clip duration, consistent aspect ratio, prompt enhancement off and explicit character references. Verify selected keyframes before animating. Save completed assets and record exact identities; repair only defective scenes and reuse completed assets when resuming.

Place narration in its approved order, with any presenter clips included once and without duplicate narration. Align visuals to actual audio boundaries with no gaps. Mute generated scene speech beneath narration while preserving intended presenter speech. If footage is short, add coverage. If footage extends beyond the final audio endpoint, cut only the excess video at that endpoint, leaving the audio unselected and unchanged. Match endpoints within frame precision.

Save the project and verify completed asset status, scene order, readable text, rights/credits and timeline endpoints. Do not claim to have listened or checked lip-sync without doing so. Avoid routine autoplay; playback review can be requested as a separate review step. Record what was and was not verified. Export using the available controls when production is authorized; repository setup alone does not authorize paid generation or publication. Keep public gallery sharing off unless requested.

## 6. Upload and repurposing package

Prepare final title plus 2–3 alternatives, thumbnail concept, description with source/asset credits, chapters from the final export timings, search phrases, pinned-comment draft, playlist placement, end-screen destination and next-video recommendation. Use only verified destination links. Drafts are not publication; publish only on explicit instruction.

Plan 3–5 Shorts, an audio/podcast adaptation, blog/newsletter material, quote posts and 2–4 long-form follow-ups as appropriate. Preserve quotation context, attribution and links to the flagship. Bridge Florence topics such as fear, prosperity, spoken word and intuition into relevant existing Soul Whisperer material without inventing product or playlist links.

## 7. Prototype learning

Record actual tool limits, narration duration, scene coverage, defects, production effort and changes needed. Keep Florence-specific findings in its project folder. Promote reusable changes only after they have been tested; never edit the reference baseline.
